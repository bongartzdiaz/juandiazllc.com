# Pitch template — operator field report

Outlet editors get ~30 pitches a day. The ones that land share a
shape: a specific claim, a specific dataset, a specific author, a
specific deadline. This template is that shape.

Do NOT pitch a column. Pitch a piece with one number the editor
can put in the headline.

---

## Subject line

```
Op-ed pitch: <one-line claim with a number> — data from <N> installations
```

Examples that work:
- `Op-ed pitch: salderingsregeling abolition will add 3.1 years to
  rooftop-solar payback — data from 47 NL installations`
- `Op-ed pitch: Solarpaket I moved DE peak-demand windows 90 min —
  data from 12 C&I sites`

Examples that don't work:
- `Thoughts on the energy transition` (no number, no claim)
- `Partnership opportunity` (not a pitch)

## Body — 4 paragraphs, nothing more

**1. The claim, in one sentence.** State the finding. No hedging.
No throat-clearing. One sentence. If you can't compress it to one
sentence, you don't have a piece yet.

**2. Why it's defensible.** Name the dataset. How many sites, over
what window, under what assumptions. If the editor's lawyer asks
"can we publish this number?", the answer should be in this
paragraph. Two sentences max.

**3. Why operators care.** Not "why readers care" — why the
operator running a business tomorrow morning changes a decision
after reading it. One sentence. If you can't answer this, it's a
think-piece, not a field report.

**4. Why me.** One sentence on authorship. "I'm the operator for X.
Here's the GitHub/deployment/contracts trail." Link one artifact.
No CV.

## Attachments

- Link to the raw dataset (Notion page, Google Sheet, public
  Postgres dump — whatever lets the editor audit before publishing).
- 2-3 chart images at 1600×900 px, captioned in the same language
  as the outlet.
- One author headshot, square, 800×800 px, neutral background.

## Turnaround promise

Tell the editor when you'll deliver the draft:

```
Draft: <ISO date, within 5 working days of go>
Length: 900–1,100 words, NL/DE/EN as the outlet prefers
Revisions: 1 round, <48 hours>
```

Editors want a reliable contributor more than a brilliant one.
Deliver on the date you promised.

## Do NOT

- Do not attach a slide deck.
- Do not use "quick" or "just" in the subject line.
- Do not say "thought this might be interesting" — say what the
  claim is.
- Do not pitch the same claim to two outlets in the same week.
  Exclusivity is leverage; burn it once.
- Do not follow up within 72 hours. After 72 hours, one short
  nudge. After that, move on.

## One-line check before sending

> Reading only the subject line + paragraph 1, would an operator
> forward this to their team? If no, rewrite.
